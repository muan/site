The other day I chatted with someone Italian, who lives in Berlin but would rather not, about people’s manners in US East and West, and various parts of Europe. He liked the West Coast’s superficial nicenesses, as it makes daily life easy, and he prefers Scandinavian directness without judgement over Berliner directness with. And perhaps naturally, he favors southern Europeans’ direct yet warm approach the most.

We did agree on liking Finnish and Norwegian’s tampered attitudes all around. Very easy to hang with in general.

However I was considering how I find Spanish and Italian a little bit intimidating, despite the warmth. I said it’s a bit like dogs. At some level I am scared because of my own inability to reciprocate. He understood but also said the Italians are less overwhelming, while the Spanish tend to be more golden retriever. My Spanish friends tend to be very cynical and that helps.

Then I realized it’s perhaps because of how it is expected that southern Europeans be warm, that when I don’t receive it, it becomes especially alienating.

So in a way living in Berlin it’s a bit like tampering one’s expectations so low that it makes any gram of niceness a pleasant surprise. It’s hard to feel more alienated.

This is all incredibly reductive, and he is the first Italian person I got to talk to long. Personally I really dislike how people get to know one person from a place then think that’s everyone from that place.

I would say it is more “the social norm” felt, opposed to people. And that social norm perceived is heavily skewed by the object and context of the perception.

As expected, I have met many people who moved to Berlin all excited and wide-eyed to live the Berlin life, and they left before the first lease was up. 

And then there are the transplants who hate living in Berlin so much that every year was supposed to be their last, yet they’re going 15 years strong.
